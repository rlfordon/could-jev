# Sweep 2026-10-06

This is the first sweep, so it sets the baseline. The model is unchanged (`jev-1.13.0`). The community is far bigger than the skill assumed: an arXiv survey (2609.30216) counts about 2,170 public Jev repos, and [awesome-typesafe-jev](https://github.com/AbdelStark/awesome-typesafe-jev) lists about 250 projects.

## Official changes

**No change** to the model version, price, rate limits, token budget, question types, or option and level limits.

**Jaggedness list:** it now has 9 items (last reviewed 2026-10-02).
- Two items were missing from the factsheet: #7, contradictory instructions and criteria; and #9, generation.
- Sub-points added: count by asking one Noul per item and summing in code; don't interpolate magnitudes from a Score; numeric encodings do worse than names.
- The "calibration" item in the factsheet is relabeled as a field report, since it isn't on TypeSafe's list.

**Run-to-run variance (documented on 1.13):** the mean per-question SD is about 0.01, but a Noul near the line can cross 0.5, and Choice labels repeated about 91% of the time.

**Access:**
- **Pydantic AI Gateway:** a new route.
- **Vercel:** a generic Decision API, plus the AI SDK provider `@ai-sdk/typesafe-ai`, which rounds probabilities to 2 decimals. It also has built-in decision fallbacks that rerun a decision on an LLM.
- **OpenRouter:** an alternate Decisions endpoint. Separately, `typesafe/jev-router` is a model router built on Jev, not the decision API.
- **n8n:** a community node.
- **`system-one-adapter-python`:** serves the same API from an LLM, for A/B tests.

**SDKs:**
- Python `typesafe-sdk` 0.7.2. Its 0.7.0 release was breaking (moved to pydantic, added `response_model=`).
- JS `@typesafe-ai/sdk` 0.6.0. Also breaking: `Score.criteria` is now an ordered list.

**Other API facts:** `GET /v1/models`, `529 Overloaded`, JSON instructions, published confidence formulas, no fine-tuning, and zero data retention for enterprise only.

**Most cookbook numbers come from jev-1.12, not 1.13.**

**Official cookbooks added to the factsheet:** self-consistency, confidence back-off to a parent label, structure recovery, entity alignment, RAG passage triage, routing and function calling, and Jev answers as features for a gradient-boosted model.

**Benchmark:** on WorkflowEvals, Jev scored about 68% vs. about 74% for a frontier LLM, at a tiny fraction of the cost.

**Roadmap:** nothing dated. TypeSafe says jaggedness items "will be fixed in later versions", and images, audio and video are "not yet" supported.

## Variants folded into existing patterns

- **#3 Funnel:**
  - jev-certify's conformal thresholds auto-routed 85% of queries with 2% loss.
  - Southbridge entity resolution matched 199/200. This counters the "dedupe underwhelming" note in #18.
- **#4 Census:** BTK judged 1,204 pages in under 3 minutes; Jev Wrapped profiles posts month by month.
- **#7 Beam descent:** used for retrieval as well as labels.
  - jevgrep: 8/10 SWE-bench tasks, cost down 26%.
  - jev-doc-search descends a table of contents.
  - jevbox.
- **#9 Attribute layer:** weighted Scores beat a direct question on NLI, but false positives on hard benign cases rose 25x.
- **#10 Spotlight:** ask the user back when the top two options are close.
- **#15 Heat map:** jev-skip paints sponsor segments on the YouTube seek bar (77%); grep by meaning (jgrep, jev.nvim).
- **#17 Extraction:** span named-entity recognition by nominating, verifying, then fixing boundaries (74 strict F1 averaged over 12 benchmarks).
- **#20 Output check:** pytest-jev.
- **#21 Tripwires:** jev-permission-gate made 1 unsafe allow in 3,664 risky calls; wakegate.
- **#22 Saturation:** used as a search stopping rule (the Zilliz test, Lyzr's AUROC 0.90).

## Rules changed

- **Promoted to a rule of thumb: "Request shape changes answers."** Three independent signals back it: documented run-to-run variance, a batch of 40 rows flipping a ranking gate (jev-orderby-bench), and the earlier question-set observation.
- **Strengthened: "Fit thresholds on your own labels."** It now adds "and refit when the data shifts", because thresholds didn't carry over between datasets (Janus) and a gate missed its target by 3.6x under a prevalence shift (jev-certify).
- **Hunch updated: "Calibration may lean by type."** Independent reports show poor Choice calibration (dice odds, ChaosNLI, AITA vote splits). Noul *under*confidence isn't confirmed elsewhere.

## Reported failures and limits

- **Calibration:**
  - Choice ignores stated odds: it put 83% on die face 1.
  - Total-variation distance was close to random.
  - Overconfident on contested NLI items.
  - Confidence barely tracked annotator vote splits (r 0.06–0.24).
- **Beaten by cheap specialists:**
  - DeBERTa on prompt injection: 89.0 vs. 86.4, and about 6x faster (Red Hat).
  - A 10-shot embedding classifier on Banking77: 0.875 vs. 0.801.
  - Voyage on reranking.
  - GPT-5-mini by 1–4 points on multi-hop.
- **Bias:** with "unknown" removed, it picked the stereotype 79% of the time on KoBBQ.
- **Pruning context** dropped information needed later (pi-jev-context).

## Interesting but unproven (report only)

- Jevtown: 10,000 personas, with reactions sampled from the probabilities.
- Sampling Jev's distribution as a generator: 1 Million Emojis, Sprite Fusion levels.
- No hindsight leakage on earnings outcomes (AUC 0.506 vs. 0.587). This might make it useful for backtests, but it's provisional until 2027-01.
- Jev as a soft predicate in Prolog, Hunch, or SQL WHERE clauses.
- Hysteresis thresholds (jevbooks "Thermostat").
- LeJudge: Jev probabilities as a planner's cost.
- Open-weight clones (Kev, jevos) trail Jev by about 3–11 points. They might matter for confidential data someday.
